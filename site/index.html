What is CVD?

CVD means Chemical Vapour Deposition. Gas or vapour reactions form a solid film on a substrate; volatile products and unused feed leave through the exhaust. These films can insulate, connect or protect chip features, or form semiconductor layers.

How CVD works · gas roles · CVD types

How the film forms

  1. Prepare the surface. Load the prepared wafer and establish the recipe’s temperature and pressure.
  2. Deliver the chemicals. Feed precursor and co-reactants. Liquid TEOS is delivered as vapour.
  3. Activate the reaction. Heat drives thermal CVD. In these PECVD examples, RF plasma assists the chemistry while the substrate is heated.
  4. Grow the solid film. Species reach and react at the substrate; film-forming atoms enter the layer.
  5. Remove reaction products. Exhaust removes unused feed and volatile products during growth. Loading and finishing stages below are illustrative.

What each term means

Wafer / substrate
The supporting surface for device fabrication, usually silicon in these examples. The GaN preset uses a prepared GaN template and buffer on sapphire.
Precursor
A starting compound that supplies atoms for the film.
Co-reactant
Another chemical that participates in forming the deposit.
Carrier / diluent
Moves or dilutes a feed. Ar and He are used in the cited TiN and TEOS examples.
Plasma
An ionised gas containing free electrons, ions and neutral species.
RF power
Radio-frequency electrical power used to sustain the discharge in these PECVD presets.
Deposit / film
The solid layer left on the substrate. Its material and properties determine its function.
By-product
Another reaction product; volatile products can leave through the exhaust.

Polysilicon and epitaxial silicon

Polysilicon

Polycrystalline silicon contains many crystal grains, with differing orientations and grain boundaries.

The silane LPCVD example forms a poly-Si film. Suitable doping and patterning allow gate-electrode and resistor applications.

Epitaxial silicon

Silicon homoepitaxy grows a single-crystal silicon layer whose crystal orientation follows the underlying crystalline silicon wafer.

The SiCl₄/H₂ example illustrates growth of this crystalline device layer on a prepared silicon surface.

Structure and purity describe different properties. Polycrystalline refers to grain structure. Purification of metallurgical silicon can also produce high-purity bulk polysilicon for wafer manufacture.

This lab illustrates a deposited thin poly-Si film; it does not model bulk feedstock purification.

One clear example · silane / LPCVD

SiH4 (gas)→Si (solid)+2H2 (gas)

The net equation puts silicon in the film and hydrogen in the gas phase. A different co-reactant can give a different film from the same precursor.

What is PECVD?

Plasma-enhanced chemical vapour deposition (PECVD) uses plasma-created reactive species to form a film at the substrate. Plasma activation often permits lower wafer temperatures than thermal CVD.

One documented RF example is 13.56 MHz; equipment can use other frequencies or combinations. The silane oxide example uses 300 °C. The TEOS example starts at 350 °C, an endpoint of the cited 350–400 °C patent range for its 300 mm equipment.

What is MOCVD?

Metal-organic chemical vapour deposition (MOCVD), also called MOVPE for epitaxial growth, uses metal-organic precursors to grow semiconductor layers. The GaN example uses heat, TMGa and ammonia.

Ga(CH3)3+NH3→GaN (solid)+3CH4

TMGa supplies Ga and ammonia supplies N. This net equation summarises many reaction steps; CH₄ represents an idealised net product, not a measured exhaust composition. A carrier gas transports TMGa vapour from a temperature-controlled bubbler.

The preset illustrates the final GaN layer of Liang et al.’s sample T2: 1050 °C, 200 Torr, on a prepared buffer/template on sapphire. GaN is a compound semiconductor used in LED heterostructures. Choose TMGa in the simulator to explore it.

Equations, assumptions & source

J = kₘ(Cᵦ − Cₛ) = kₛCₛ, with kₘ = D/δ. Therefore Cₛ/Cᵦ = 1/(1 + Dₐ), and J/(kₘCᵦ) = Dₐ/(1 + Dₐ).

With constant diffusivity and no gas-phase reaction, C(z)/Cᵦ = Cₛ/Cᵦ + (1 − Cₛ/Cᵦ)z/δ. The plotted profile is the closed-form solution to those stated assumptions. The coordinate z points away from the wafer; J is a positive flux magnitude toward it (the signed z-flux is −D dC/dz). D has units length²/time, δ length, kₛ and kₘ length/time, C amount/volume and J amount/(area time).
